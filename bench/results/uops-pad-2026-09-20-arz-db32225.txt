local/uopspad at db32225 (N-07, the padding experiment): what an instruction costs inside a
miss-bound lookup loop, and what the loop's window is.

One `MPH3` table over n splitmix64 keys, probed in a shuffled order, with PAD padding instructions
welded into the loop between one query and the next. PAD is a const generic, so the padding unrolls
-- a `for` over a run-time count welds a compare and a branch onto every step and measures the loop
rather than the step, which is what the first cut of this harness did (0.32 ns a `nop`, five times
the real figure). Four shapes:

  nop   retires and occupies the window, needs no port and no operand
  alu   `add`s on four rotating registers, so the distance between two writes of one is four
  dep   `add`s on one register: a chain, one step a cycle whatever the window holds
  load  dependent loads inside 4 KiB, which never leaves L1

`ns/step` is the slope over the last two pad counts, which is past whatever the window absorbs.
The last line chases one dependent load at a time over a region the size of the table itself --
the latency the sweep's misses are hiding -- and divides it by the `nop` slope.

Three 100 M runs read the `nop` slope at 0.2275, 0.2317 and 0.2359 ns a step: +-1.8 %. Not on a
pinned core, and an editor was open; the agreement across runs is the only argument that it did not
matter. Ryzen 7 5800HS (8 cores, 16 MB L3, 38 GB), Fedora 44.

WHAT IT SAYS

  * An instruction added to a miss-bound lookup path costs **0.23 ns** at 100 M keys, and 0.053 ns
    at 10 M where the 2.3 MB table is resident. The loop is not paying for the instruction's own
    execution -- it is paying because the instruction displaces another query from the window, and
    the window is what buys the memory-level parallelism.
  * The table's own dependent-load latency is **85.3 ns** at 22.8 MB against **12.5 ns** at 2.3 MB,
    and the loop answers a key in 15.0 ns, so it keeps **5.7 misses in flight**.
  * Latency over the `nop` slope reads **359-375 instructions** at 100 M and 235 at 10 M. Zen 3's
    reorder buffer is 256 entries, so the 100 M figure is an upper bound rather than a reading of
    it -- some nops are eliminated at rename -- and the 10 M figure is not a window at all, the
    loop there being throughput-bound rather than window-bound.
  * A **dependent** instruction costs 1.00 ns a step against an independent one's 0.66 and a nop's
    0.23: a chain welded onto the lookup path costs what four independent instructions do, which is
    the argument against trading a table load for a longer arithmetic chain.
  * **The lever this sets for T-07.** At 300 M keys `MPH3` answers a key in 16.3 ns against
    `ptr_hash` fast's 16.2 (`mphf-misses-2026-09-20-arz-1a9003e.txt`). At 0.23 ns an instruction
    that gap is **less than half an instruction**, and MPH3 takes 15 % fewer misses to reach it.
    There is no uop-shaving win left on the single lane.

CONTROL: the sequential probe order. Probing in generation order instead of shuffled moves no cell
by more than 1 % and the slope by 3.7 % (0.2359 against 0.2275) -- the loop waits on the table, not
on the probe array, which is read sequentially either way.
