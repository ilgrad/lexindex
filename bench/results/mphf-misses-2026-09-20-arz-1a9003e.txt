bench/mphf_vs at 1a9003e, the misses-per-lookup column (N-03): three sizes, one process each,
`MPHF_VS_MISSES=1 mphf_vs <n> 1 8` -- eight build threads, one round, so no build spread; the
lookups over the full shuffled probe order at 10 M and 100 M and over 100 M keys drawn at random
with replacement at 300 M. The miss column is `PERF_COUNT_HW_CACHE_MISSES` on the timing thread
through `perf_event_open(2)`, reset and read around each of the three single-lookup passes, so it
is that pass's count and not the round's; the cell is the fastest pass's count a lookup and the
spread over the three, which is 0.0-1.1 % throughout. It counts the probe order's own stream as
well as the table: the order is read sequentially at eight keys a 64-byte line, so **0.125 of every
number below is the key fetch** -- subtract it to compare with the survey's lines-a-query.

On an editor-open machine, Tctl 60-67 C, 1-minute load 0.65-1.0 at the starts, mains, Ryzen 7
5800HS (8 cores, 16 MB L3, 38 GB), Fedora 44, ph 0.11.0 with its `wyhash` feature, ptr_hash 2.1.1.
The lookup nanoseconds here are taken with an `ioctl` either side of the pass and are **not** the
timings this page publishes; the column is off by default for that reason. Read the miss numbers.

Net of the key fetch, at 300 M: MPH3 0.946 table lines a lookup against ptr_hash fast's 1.107,
balanced's 1.117, compact's 1.100, PHast's 1.141 and PHast+'s 1.329 -- the survey quotes PtrHash
1.0 and PHast 1.1. MPH3 is the only row under one line. At 100 M, where a 24-37 MB table is partly
L3-resident, the six rows are within 1.031-1.243 and the ordering does not separate them; the gap
opens at 300 M, where nothing is resident. **So the single-lookup near-tie with ptr_hash fast at
300 M (16.3 against 16.2 ns) is not a memory result: MPH3 gets there on 15 % fewer misses
(17 % net of the key fetch), 36 % fewer bits and a build 47x faster.** What is left of that 0.1 ns is compute.
