# Plan items 37 and 62: one symbol table a shard, and where the (shard, budget) pair sits.
# lexindex 2.2-unreleased at 68f5336, measured against 42d5bc1 (one table over the whole
# index), arz (Ryzen 7 5800HS, Zen 3, 16 threads), rustc 1.98.1, 2026-09-12.
# Sizes are exact and deterministic -- one build a cell, no alternation. Latencies are the minimum
# of five rounds alternated inside one process, probes shuffled with a fixed seed, and `StringIndex`
# is the control in every timed run: this session reads about 25 % above the one the published
# latency ladder comes from, control included, so each comparison is read inside itself.

# THE CHANGE. A shard is SHARD_KEYS = 65 536 keys' worth of whole blocks. Each shard's table is
# trained on TRAIN_PIECES = 10 000 pieces sampled inside that shard alone, in runs of 256 keys; the
# tables are trained in parallel; the blob carries them after the header, which names the shard in
# blocks, each behind its own u32 length. A lookup divides the block number by the shard to pick
# its table -- one division, no memory access the lookup did not already make.

# flat between 65 536 and 32 768 and both ends rise for opposite reasons: too few shards and one
# table covers neighbourhoods that share nothing; too many and 900 bytes of table a shard is the
# whole budget (0.060 B/key at 16 384, against a `words` index of 2.90). 65 536 over 32 768 because
# the two are level on the mean while 65 536 costs the dictionary 0.21 % where 32 768 costs 0.95 %,
# and because the build at ten million keys rises 5 % rather than 16 %: equal work is not equal
# time, a shard has a fixed cost of its own.
